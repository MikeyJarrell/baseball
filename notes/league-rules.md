# League rules the scorebook app depends on

The Diamond Dogs play in the San Diego Adult Baseball League (SDABL), 18A division. Rule numbers refer to the SDABL rule book dated April 21, 2026, kept with a text extract in `~/Dropbox/baseball/reference/`. Rules below are summarized; check the rule book itself before treating a summary as settled.

## Game length

- Nine innings (9.1 D).
- Time limit (9.1 G): “No new inning shall begin after 3 hours from the actual start time of the game.” The clock starts at the actual start, which the app takes as the first pitch. The plate umpire keeps the official time (9.1 B), so the scorer can correct the limit. An inning that began before the limit is played to the end; the home team does not bat if it is already ahead.
- A tie when time runs out stays a tie (9.1 C, 9.1 H 1). A tie after nine innings with time left goes to extra innings (9.1 H 2).
- The time limit can be extended for a 911 call or an unplayable field; the umpire announces the new limit (9.1 I). Championship games have a limit of 3 hours 15 minutes (9.1 J).
- Mercy rule (9.13): a 12-run lead at the end of seven innings, or after six and a half with the home team ahead, ends the game. Championship games use 15 runs.
- Rain or darkness: official after five innings, or four and a half with the home team ahead (9.1 E, 9.16).

## Batting order

- A team may bat any number of players, and at least nine must bat when nine or more are present (9.2 A, 9.5 A).
- Batters may be added at any time, at the bottom of the order (9.5 B).
- A player lost with no eligible substitute leaves a “batting hole” that counts as an out each time it comes up; the order may not be compressed (9.2 B, 9.5 C).
- Batters do not have to play defense (9.5 C), and players can play defense without batting (3.5 B 2). The batting order and the fielders are separate lists.

## Substitutions and re-entry

- A substitute who is removed cannot re-enter (9.6 B).
- A starter may be removed and re-entered any number of times, always in the original batting spot, and only after the substitute has actually batted at least once (9.9). A pinch runner who replaces a starter or substitute is in the batting order once on the base (9.9).
- Defensive substitution is free at every position except pitcher (9.12 A).
- A pitcher, once removed, may come back to pitch only once per game (9.12 B 1), and must finish the current batter before being removed unless injured (9.12 B 2).

## Designated non-runners

- Declared on the lineup card before the game; no player can be made a non-runner after it starts (9.4 C, 9.11 C). The 18+ divisions allow two (9.11 J).
- Replacement is optional: a non-runner who has just batted and reached may have another player run, before the next pitch (9.11 A, 9.11 D). No use-it-or-lose-it (9.11 I).
- The runner is the last recorded out, a player and not a batting spot (9.11 D, 9.11 H). In the first inning before any out, it is the last batter on the lineup card (9.11 E). Being on deck is no excuse; being at bat is, and then the runner is the last recorded out before the current batter (9.11 F, G).
- A non-runner replaced by a pinch runner instead leaves the batting order under the ordinary substitution rule (9.11 M). If the last-recorded-out runner is then substituted for right away, the manager may substitute for that runner and keep the non-runner in the order (9.11 N).
- The run scored by the runner for a designated non-runner is credited to the designated non-runner (Mikey, October 3, 2026).

## Scorekeeping

- The home team’s scorebook is the official book (9.14 A).
