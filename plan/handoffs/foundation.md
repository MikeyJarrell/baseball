## How a 5-4-3 double play is recorded

One `ballInPlay` event. The play has `batterResult: out`, `fielders: [5, 4, 3]`, `multiOut: doublePlay`, and two runner moves: the runner from first to `out` at second with `outBy: [5, 4]`, and the batter from home to `out` at first with `outBy: [4, 3]`. The last fielder in each `outBy` gets the putout, the others assists. The comment at the top of `Model/Play.swift` walks through it.

## No league rule is enforced

The league is casual, and Mikey’s principle is that the app never enforces a rule. Each rule either asks the scorer a question or lists choices; whatever the scorer records stands.

- **The time limit and the run rule ask “Is the game over?”** (Mikey’s decision) `Situation.gameOverQuestion` is set (`regulation`, `timeLimit` or `runRule`) when a rule says the game could end. The game ends only with the scorer’s `endOfGame` event. Answering “keep playing” records a `keepPlaying` event, which clears the question; that reason is never asked again in the game (`Situation.declinedEndings`), and innings after it are ordinary innings with no last-inning warning. Because the answer is an event, undo and replay remember it. The scenario `keep-playing-past-time-limit` checks it.
- **The runner for a designated non-runner** is a suggestion (`Situation.runnerChoice.suggestedRunner`); any runner the scorer picks is recorded, and the designated non-runner may run themselves, which records nothing.
- **Re-entry and pitcher re-entry** are eligibility lists (`TeamState.eligibleToReenter`, `eligibleToPitchAgain`) for the substitution screens to show; nothing is blocked.

## Decisions every builder needs

- **Ids are readable text** (`"d3"`, `"m1"`; random codes for things made in the app). Batting spots are counted from 0 everywhere (`batterIndex`, `nextBatterIndex`, `spotIndex`).
- **The batting order and the defense are separate lists** [9.5 C, 3.5 B 2]. `GameTeam` has `battingOrder` (player ids, any number of hitters), `defense` (position to player, one player per position; a position nobody plays is left out, for an unknown opponent or a team playing short-handed), `designatedNonRunners` and `roster`, with `bench` worked out from them. A player may bat only, field only, or both; nobody needs an extra-hitter or designated-hitter label. In the situation, `BattingSlot` holds who has batted in a spot and `TeamState.fielders` holds who is where, with `position(of:)` to look a player up. `Substitution` carries `batting` changes (pinch hitter, pinch runner, replacement), `defense` assignments, and `leavesField` for a fielder who leaves with nobody replacing them (Mikey’s decision: a position may be left empty); the defense check and position switches touch only the defense. `AddHitter` only adds to the batting order. `PitchingChange` changes only the defense unless `takesBattingSpotOf` says whose batting spot the new pitcher takes. Fielding stats stay with each player through the play log’s fielders, and every player who took a position gets a fielding line with `appearances` (position and innings), even with all zeros, so a player who fields without batting can show they took part, for playoff eligibility (Mikey’s decision, [3.5 B 2]).
- **Game rules live in the setup** (`GameSetup.rules`, a `GameRules`): nine innings, a 180-minute time limit, the 12-run rule from the seventh inning, two designated non-runners per team. The time limit is set per game (Mikey’s decision): three hours by default, with `GameRules.championship` offering 195 minutes and 15 runs [9.1 J, 9.13 B]. Rule 8.1 C, which says championship games have no time limit and playoff ties at the limit keep going, conflicts; the comment on `timeLimitMinutes` notes it. A setup file may leave `rules` out for the league’s rules. The setup also carries `scheduledStart` (9:00 in the morning by default, `GameSetup.nineInTheMorning(on:)`).
- **The time limit** runs from the first pitch [9.1 G]. Before it, `Situation.timeLimitEndsAt` is the scheduled start plus the limit. The scorer can set the umpire’s end time with a `timeLimitChange` event [9.1 B, 9.1 I]. The situation reports `isLastInning` (the limit passed during an inning that began before it) and raises the `timeLimit` question when that inning ends, or after its top half if the home team leads. A tie at the time limit stays a tie; a tie after nine with time left goes to extra innings with no question. **That inning is the last inning for walk-off purposes** (Mikey’s decision): the moment the home team takes the lead in its bottom half, the question is asked, and only the winning run is suggested to score (all of them on a home run), as in the bottom of the ninth. `Situation.isWalkOffInning` says when this applies, and `PlayPreview.endsGame` reports it. **The live clock belongs to the rules engine** (Mikey’s decision: one copy): `RulesEngine.clock(for:at:)` carries the engine’s stored decisions forward between events (time left, last inning, whether to ask) and never asks anything once the game is final. The stand-ins use `simpleClock`, which only repeats the stored facts.
- **The run rule** raises the `runRule` question at the end of the seventh inning or any later one with a lead of the margin, or after the top half with the home team ahead by it [9.13]. Only when a half-inning ends, never in the middle of one (Mikey’s decision, following “at the end of seven innings”).
- **`isRegulationComplete`** now means an official game if rain or darkness stops play: five innings, or four and a half with the home team ahead [9.1 E]. Whether the game is over is the separate `gameOverQuestion`.
- **A batting hole** [9.2 B, 9.5 C] is a `battingHole` event marking a spot whose player is lost with nobody to replace them. Each time the spot comes up, `Situation.isBattingHoleUp` is true and the app records an `automaticOut` event straight away. It is an ordinary event, saved, undone and edited like any other (Mikey’s decision). In the play log it is nobody’s plate appearance, gives no putout or assist to any fielder (its `OutMade` names the hole’s last player with no putout), counts toward the pitcher’s outs and innings, and is not a “recorded out” for the designated non-runner rule. The slot shows `isHole`. A later batting change into the spot fills it.
- **Designated non-runners** [9.11]: declared before the game in `GameTeam.designatedNonRunners`, from the batting order or the bench (Mikey’s decision, [9.4 C]); the batting slot shows `isDesignatedNonRunner` for whoever holds it. When one reaches base, `Situation.runnerChoice` is set and stays open until the next pitch, ball in play or intentional walk (Mikey’s decision, [9.11 A 3, D]), so a pitching change in between does not close it, with the suggested runner by the league’s rule: the team’s last recorded out (a player); if that player is now at bat, the last recorded out before them; in the first inning before any out, the last batter in the batting order; never the designated non-runner themself. The rules engine gives the same answer through `suggestedRunner(for:in:situation:)`; both stand-ins answer it. The runner is the `runnerForNonRunner` event; running themselves needs no event. A pinch runner for a designated non-runner is an ordinary substitution and takes the non-runner out of the batting order [9.11 M]. In the sample game Webb, the last out, runs for Quinn and is caught stealing; the caught stealing is on Webb’s own line.
- **Re-entry** [9.6 B, 9.9]: only starters, always to their original spot, only after their substitute (or a later one) has batted; a removed substitute never. `TeamState.eligibleToReenter` lists starters and their spots. **Pitcher re-entry** [9.12 B]: a removed pitcher may pitch again once per game; `eligibleToPitchAgain` lists them, and drops a pitcher who has already come back once. A pitcher removed in the middle of a batter is allowed only for an injury [9.12 B 2]; the 3-1 pitching-change scenario says so.
- **Pitcher warm-up music** replaces the between-innings playlist: a `Player` may have a `warmUpSongID`, and `WalkUpAudioPlayer.playWarmUp(for:)` plays our pitcher’s song when our team takes the field, until stopped or the first pitch. Nothing plays for the opponent. The playlist is gone from `SongLibrary` and the audio promise.
- **Rosters live in the game setup and change without events.** Naming “Batter 4”, or giving their number 22 a name, edits the player in `GameSetup` and holds for the whole game. A sheet that creates a player on the spot hands back an `EnteredEvent`: the event plus `newPlayers`, which the app adds to the roster before recording the event.
- **A dropped third strike rides on the strike-three pitch** (`Pitch.droppedThirdStrike`, a `Play`). When the scorer taps the dropped-third-strike button, the wiring replaces the strike-three event with the same pitch carrying the play. `Situation.droppedThirdStrikePossible` says when to show the button.
- **A dropped foul pop-up** is two events: the foul pitch, then `errorWithNoAdvance`.
- **After a third out, the situation is already the next half-inning** with no outs and `isBetweenHalfInnings` true.
- **An error is listed once per play or runner action**, in `errors`; each runner it helped has `advancedOnError` set. **A stolen base is always one base**; any bases past it came from an error.
- **Rate stats are formulas in Model** (`BattingCounts.battingAverage`, `onBasePercentage`, `sluggingPercentage`; `PitchingCounts.inningsPitchedText` and the per-inning rates) and print with `formattedRate`. Stats fills in the counts.
- **Box score lines**: every player who held a batting spot gets a line, even with no plate appearances (the relief pitchers), with substitutes under the player they replaced; a player who only batted has no fielding position in `positions`. Every player who played a position gets a fielding line with their `appearances`. Someone who ran for a designated non-runner without a batting spot of their own gets a `pr` line under the non-runner (Mikey’s decision; written on `BattingLine`), though under the league’s rule the runner is normally a player already in the order, who keeps everything on their own line.
- **A manual adjustment that changes the score** (Mikey’s decision): the runs belong to that team in the inning the adjustment leaves the game in, credited to no player and charged to no pitcher, so the line score always adds up to the final score. The rules engine reports them on the play log entry as `adjustedRuns`.
- **Runners left on base** (Mikey’s decision): the runners on base whenever a half-inning ends, including the last half-inning however the game ends. In the play log that is the `after` runners of every third-out entry plus those of the end-of-game entry.
- **Walk-up songs fade by themselves** (Mikey’s decision) after `AppSettings.walkUpSongSeconds`, 12 seconds by default, counted after any announcer introduction; a tap stops it sooner. The audio player takes it as `walkUpLength`; a song’s own `playLength` overrides it.
- **Storage lists** (`loadTeams`, `listGames`, `finishedGames`) return a `LoadedList`: the items that read cleanly plus a `DamagedFile` for each file that did not.
- **The rules engine** also suggests runner outcomes for a runner action: `suggestRunnerAction(_:runners:in:)`.
- **Walk-up audio** is `Observable`, with an `onPlaybackEnded` hook; `importSongs` and `importIntroduction` copy in the background and `addSongs` puts the songs in the library.
- **Notation** in the sample game and scenarios: `1B 7`, `2B 9`, `HR 7`, `6-3`, `F8`, `L6`, `P6`, `3U`, `5-4-3 DP`, `L4-3 DP`, `FC 4-6`, `E6`, `SF 8`, `SH 1-3`, `K`, `ꓘ`, `K 2-3`, `BB`, `IBB`, `HBP`, `SB2`, `CS2 2-6`. The scenario files pin `1B 7`, `5-4-3 DP`, `6-3`, `BB`, `K`, `K 2-3` and `E7`.
- **JSON**: always through `ScorebookJSON`. Dates are written as text and event times are kept to the whole second. `ScorebookJSON.formatVersion` is the version tag storage writes. The end-of-game event carries a copy of the final score.
